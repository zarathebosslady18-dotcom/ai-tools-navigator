The AED 5,000 test

How to measure what AI is actually saving your business — and the rule for when to stop.

Every AI engagement I run ends with the same question from the owner: "Okay — but what is it actually saving me?" Most vendors never answer, because the answer requires a measurement protocol most of them refuse to install. So here it is. It's called the AED 5,000 test, because it's calibrated to the number that matters: a single workflow saving AED 5,000 a month is a business decision, not a gadget.

Step one: the baseline is a price tag, not a vibe

Take the chosen workflow and cost the current state. Hours per week × loaded cost per hour (salary + overhead, not just salary). If quoting takes four hours a week and the loaded cost is AED 60/hour, the workflow costs AED 11,520 a year. Write that number down. It's not an estimate of AI's value — it's the ceiling on what the workflow is worth touching. If your "AI opportunity" is bigger than the workflow's cost, you're measuring fantasy.

Step two: count what the tool actually changed

After 30 days, re-run the same count. The discipline is in what you count:

  • Time removed — hours the workflow genuinely takes less now. Not "it feels faster."
  • Errors removed — misquotes, rework, re-sends. Each one had a cost in step one; count the ones that disappeared.
  • Capacity created — the subtlest one. If the workflow takes half the time, what did the freed time actually get used for? If the answer is "nothing," the saving is zero and the tool is a time-sink wearing a costume.

Convert all three to AED using step one's rates. That's your real number. In my experience it lands between 20% and 60% of the ceiling for a well-chosen workflow — and anything under the AED 5,000/month threshold gets killed, whatever the vendor says.

Why the test can't be gamed

Because it's anchored to a number computed before the purchase, using rates you supplied, measured for a fixed window, against a workflow you named. A vendor can oversell a demo; they can't oversell your baseline. And when the number is real, it does something no dashboard can: it funds the next workflow. The second deployment is approved by the first one's receipt.

That's the whole trick. AI adoption at the SME scale isn't a technology problem. It's a bookkeeping problem with a technology in it. Do the bookkeeping first, and the technology takes care of itself.
